# Context

Since around 2015/16 I've been making chiptune music on and off. But for the past few years I've been wanting to branch out and see what other kinds of music I could make. Just last year (2024) I begun using an old bit of software called FasttrackerII, which replaced the strictly chiptune software I used before, called Famitracker0CC. Famitracker0CC is a fine bit of software for chiptune composition, quite the hobbiest standard if you liked tracker-style notation. But FasttrackerII had a lot of features that weren't available in Famitracker, like the use of abitrary samples and construction of your own arbitrary waves. I thought this would be the best thing to happen to my music, but a lot of the music I made with it was quite hard to get to sound as coherent as my old chiptune music. 

It was a mixture of this and the frustration with Fasttracker's interface that made me want to move away from this, too. It was very clunky, open source sure, but it had one other _glaring_ issue. **There was no undo button.**

Stifiling the change to a better software is the restriction I must avoid spending money on this at all costs, because this will never earn me a penny. It's just for fun. For some reason this rule seems to apply just to music making of all my hobbies; one day I may break down this wall. But not today! Because about a month ago writing this, I recieved a very precious gift of an Ableton Live license... 

# Impressions

Since I've been using Ableton for a few months now I've begun to appreciate its utility over any other software so far. Even though its a version of the software below their lowest tier "_Intro_", it still has eight tracks and a very useable flexible workflow. Its great for laying down tunes and experimenting but has some features I've wanted for years like global track automation; effectively being able to apply an effect to a whole track instead of having to hand-write in the effect codes like I've been doing for over half a decade prior :/ 

# Some of the Gear, Some Idea

I am hoping to be able to use this software to replicate the styles of some of my favourite EDM artists like Virtual Riot, Kanro, Chime, and others. I know for a fact some do use this software too, albeit a more capable version. They've their own sample packs and whatnot, but the core of what they have I do too. Free plugins like Vital allow me to craft an amazing variety of synthetic sounds in a way I **vastly** prefer to what I had to work with in Fasttracker. I can make all sorts of genres of EDM basses and leads and plucks and growls, once I learn a little more about sound design I expect to spend a great many more hours in Vital setting up some strong presets.
